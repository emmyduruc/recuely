# TTS spike: Kokoro on this Mac (Task 7)

**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Machine:** Intel Core i9-9980HK (8 cores / 16 threads, no AVX-512/VNNI), 32 GB RAM, macOS 26.7 ·
**Code:** `spikes/tts/` (throwaway; `uv run python measure.py <config>`, `mapping.py`, `boundary.html`) · **Raw data:** `spikes/tts/results.jsonl`, `spikes/tts/mapping.json`

## Recommendation

1. **Use Kokoro-82M through `kokoro-onnx` with the onnx-community *timestamped* export in fp32.** It gives real per-phoneme durations at no extra cost, and fp32 is 3× faster than int8 on this CPU.
2. **Word-level highlighting is real, driven by start times.** Word starts land within 40 ms of the audible onsets. A word stays highlighted until the next one starts; phrase-final words are capped, because their end times overshoot by about 240 ms. If a chunk's timings don't map onto its words, that chunk falls back to chunk highlighting (the §B7 tiers).
3. **Prefetch the next chunk and cache audio.** A typical short chunk (≤ 8 words) synthesizes in about 1.0 s. Longer ones take 1.5–3 s, which would be a visible wait. Synthesizing chunk N+1 while the creator records chunk N hides it, and repeats are served from the cache (the §B7 cached target of ≤ 150 ms).
4. **Decide the phonemizer licence in Task 10.** `phonemizer` and `espeak-ng` are **GPL-3.0**. That's fine for a local service on this machine, but distributing the AI service would carry GPL obligations. The alternative is Kokoro's own Apache-licensed G2P, `misaki`.

## Latency, memory, size

Voice `af_heart`, median of 3 warm runs; RTF = synthesis time ÷ audio length (lower is better; < 1 is faster than real time).

| Model | Size | Load | First call | 6 words (2.3 s audio) | 13 words (3.7 s) | 27 words (7.6 s) | Peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **timestamped fp32** | 326 MB | 1.3 s | 2.2 s | **1.01 s** (RTF 0.44) | **1.54 s** (0.41) | **2.99 s** (0.39) | 1.0 GB |
| timestamped int8 | 92 MB | 1.0 s | 3.5 s | 3.53 s (1.58) | 5.21 s (1.42) | 9.97 s (1.32) | 0.65 GB |
| kokoro-onnx fp32 (no timings) | 326 MB | 1.3 s | 2.2 s | 1.02 s (0.48) | 1.56 s (0.44) | 3.07 s (0.42) | 0.95 GB |
| kokoro-onnx int8 (no timings) | 92 MB | 0.9 s | 3.4 s | 3.44 s (1.65) | 5.28 s (1.51) | 10.0 s (1.36) | 0.64 GB |

- **int8 is slower than real time here.** It relies on int8 instructions (VNNI) that this CPU doesn't have, so fp32 is the only viable option on this Mac.
- Pinning 8 intra-op threads made no difference (1.05 / 1.56 / 3.11 s).
- `create_stream` gives no earlier first audio for a 2-sentence text (2.3 s): both sentences fit in one batch. Streaming won't help; prefetching will.
- The CoreML execution provider is available but was not measured; noted as a follow-up.

## Word timings

The kokoro-onnx release model outputs audio only. The onnx-community `Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX-timestamped` export adds a `durations` output (per phoneme). The model *generates* the audio from those durations, so the timings describe the audio exactly, not approximately.

**Upstream bug:** kokoro-onnx 0.6.1 enables timings only if the model has an output named `duration`, but this export names it `durations`. The spike sets `has_timings = True` after loading. Task 10 should do the same or read the output directly, and report the issue upstream.

**Independent check against the waveform.** Silences ≥ 60 ms detected in the audio were compared with the predicted word boundaries, over 15 words and 4 internal silences:

| | fp32 | int8 |
|---|---|---|
| Word start vs end of silence: max / median | **39.6 / 37.5 ms** | 46.4 / 39.6 ms |
| Phrase-final word end vs start of silence: max overshoot | 238 ms | 240 ms |

Word starts meet the ≤ 50 ms bar (H-10). End times of words before a pause include the silence, so highlighting uses start times only.

**Mapping timings onto the script's words.** Each script word is phonemized on its own to learn how many spoken words it becomes; the sentence's spoken words must add up. Results over 10 lines, including all E1 spoken lines:

| Result | Lines |
|---|---|
| Maps exactly | 8 of 10, incl. every ordinary script line, contractions, "Dr.", "$20", "15%", "21st", "part 2", an emoji |
| Doesn't add up | "Use 3 screws, 2 brackets and **1.5 metres** of wood." · "We start at **10:30, e.g.** right after lunch, in the U.S. office." |

A mismatch is detectable (the counts differ), so the chunk falls back to chunk highlighting and nothing is faked (§A6.4).

**Product finding:** Kokoro reads emoji aloud ("👋" → "waving hand"). Whether script emoji should be spoken, skipped, or stripped from `spokenText` is a Task 12 review-UI question.

## Platform risks

- **onnxruntime 1.30 has no Intel-Mac wheels**; 1.23.2 is pinned. A future onnxruntime can't be used on this machine, and ONNX-based STT/VAD in Task 8 has the same limit.
- PyTorch no longer ships Intel-Mac builds, which is why the ONNX route was used instead of the `kokoro` (torch) package.

## Hypotheses

- **H-10** (Kokoro gives word timings) → **Validated.** Via the timestamped export, word starts are ≤ 40 ms from the audio; use start times; fall back to chunk highlighting when the word mapping doesn't add up (2 of 10 test lines).
- **H-11** (a sentence in ≤ 1.2 s) → **Partly refuted.** Short chunks of ≤ 8 words take about 1.0 s; 13 words take 1.5 s and 27 words 3.0 s (fp32). int8 is refuted on this CPU. Mitigation: prefetch the next chunk and cache.
